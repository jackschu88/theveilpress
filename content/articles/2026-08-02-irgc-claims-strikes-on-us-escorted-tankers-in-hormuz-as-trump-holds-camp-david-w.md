Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said Friday it struck two oil tankers attempting to transit the Strait of Hormuz under U.S. aerial escort, as President Donald Trump convened his cabinet at Camp David over a war that has stalled, closed the waterway, and pushed energy prices higher ahead of the November midterms.[[1]](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jul/31/iran-strikes-tankers-us-escort-hormuz-strait-trump-gathers-cabinet)

The IRGC described the vessels as “non-compliant,” saying they were “struck and brought to a halt” after trying an “undeclared route” under U.S. escort. Four other tankers, it said, changed course and returned to prior positions. Iranian state-linked outlets framed the ships as misled by U.S. Central Command and ignoring repeated Iranian warnings. Those claims are Iranian assertions; independent confirmation of hits, damage, and the exact escort posture was not established in the initial reporting.[[2]](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/irans-irgc-says-it-hit-2-tankers-forced-4-others-to-turn-back-in-strait-of-hormuz/4014805)

### Competing corridors

The incident sits on a fractured navigation system that has replaced peacetime transit through the strait. Iran and the United States have promoted rival routes. Tehran has attacked shipping that uses a southern path close to Oman’s coast; Washington has enforced measures against vessels linked to Iranian traffic and ports. The Guardian reported the strait has been almost completely closed to traffic since fighting between the U.S. and Iran resumed about two weeks earlier.[[1]](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jul/31/iran-strikes-tankers-us-escort-hormuz-strait-trump-gathers-cabinet)

That split is not new to this round. Earlier in July, the U.S. Navy–linked Joint Maritime Information Center raised the Hormuz threat level to “severe” after Iranian attacks on ships using a southern corridor associated with U.S. protection. Maritime analysts described a northern route under Iranian registration pressure and a southern corridor hugging Oman, with the traditional mid-strait channel avoided after mining. Pre-war, roughly a fifth of traded oil and gas moved through Hormuz; wartime volumes have remained far below that baseline even when interim arrangements briefly increased traffic.[[3]](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/07/iran-strait-hormuz-oil-tanker-lng.html)

On Thursday, before the IRGC’s Friday claim, CENTCOM said U.S. forces had redirected 24 commercial vessels and disabled and boarded four others “to ensure full compliance,” while rejecting Iranian warnings that navigation was unsafe and blaming IRGC action as the primary threat to civilian crews. The IRGC answered by telling shipowners and insurers not to heed CENTCOM statements.[[2]](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/irans-irgc-says-it-hit-2-tankers-forced-4-others-to-turn-back-in-strait-of-hormuz/4014805)

### Prices, bases, and a five-month war

Crude pushed past $90 a barrel on Thursday after the U.S. struck Iran in response to Iranian targeting of U.S. bases in Jordan, according to The Guardian. Energy disruption has kept U.S. gasoline and grocery costs elevated—the political core of the Camp David agenda as Republicans face midterms. Trump had projected a short campaign; the conflict, launched with U.S. and Israeli strikes on February 28, has now run about five months.[[1]](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jul/31/iran-strikes-tankers-us-escort-hormuz-strait-trump-gathers-cabinet)

A Reuters/Ipsos poll released days earlier found roughly one in three Americans supporting the war—the lowest reading in that series since the conflict’s early period—with 69% saying Trump has not clearly explained U.S. goals. His approval sat at 37% in the same survey. On Thursday the Senate rejected, 49–50, a war-powers measure that would have directed withdrawal from hostilities with Iran absent explicit congressional authorization. Three Republicans—Susan Collins, Lisa Murkowski, and Rand Paul—joined Democrats in support; the vote failed.[[4]](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/just-one-three-americans-back-iran-war-most-unsure-trumps-goals-reutersipsos-2026-07-27/)

The Guardian also reported that major U.S. energy firms have posted sharply higher profits during the conflict, citing ExxonMobil profits doubling and Chevron more than five times the prior-year period. Those figures are outlet-reported financial outcomes tied to the war’s price spike, not proof of policy design.

### Regional spillover the same day

Kuwait’s defence ministry said it shot down Iranian drones aimed at “vital and military facilities,” with no casualties reported. In southern Lebanon, the Israeli military carried out a large detonation near Beaufort Castle, saying it targeted Hezbollah tunnels and infrastructure; the blast was heard across the south and left ash over nearby towns. Lebanese officials called it a ceasefire violation; Israel said it was securing its northern residents.[[1]](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jul/31/iran-strikes-tankers-us-escort-hormuz-strait-trump-gathers-cabinet)

White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt had described the Camp David cabinet session in advance as “a lot of fun and something different for the cabinet to experience together.” The substance on the table is harder: a strait still contested by force, public support thin, congressional patience fraying, and no verified reopening of normal Hormuz traffic.

What is established is the IRGC claim, the dual-route enforcement pattern, the price move past $90 after the Jordan-linked U.S. strikes, the poll and Senate numbers, and parallel incidents in Kuwait and Lebanon. What is not established from open reporting is independent battle-damage assessment on the two tankers, the precise composition of any U.S. “air escort,” or whether Friday’s action changes the underlying stalemate over who sets the rules of passage.
