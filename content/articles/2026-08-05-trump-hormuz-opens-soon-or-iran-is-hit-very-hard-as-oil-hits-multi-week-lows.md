## Trump ties Hormuz reopening to force as oil drops on deal hopes

President Donald Trump said the Strait of Hormuz will open "very soon" or Iran will be "hit very hard," pairing a near-term deal forecast with an explicit military threat as crude futures fell to multi-week lows on expectations of restored tanker traffic.[[1]](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cpw9v0gnzxwo)

In a Fox News interview Tuesday night from Southern California, Trump framed the outcome as binary: "The Strait is going to be open very soon, or they're going to hit very hard, and then the Strait is going to be open." He added that if Iranian officials "back out again, they are going to get hit really hard," claiming "very good discussions" were under way and that he had "no choice."[[2]](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/iran-war-donald-trump-us-denies-negotiations-begin-strikes-called-off/)

Separately he told reporters a Hormuz arrangement could land "tomorrow or the next day" after what he called a "very good day" for negotiators. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC there was "a chance we may have a deal" by Wednesday or Thursday.[[3]](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/iran-oman-draft-waterway-deal-025457395.html)

### Markets price de-escalation, not closure

Oil sold off hard on the diplomacy signal. On Tuesday, Brent crude fell roughly 5% and closed near $79.36 a barrel; WTI settled about 5.7% lower at $75.77 — the lowest closes for both since July 10, according to market reporting that tracked the move to a three-week low. BBC reporting put Brent under $80 on the same session as supply-disruption risk was marked down.[[4]](https://www.wsj.com/finance/commodities-futures/oil-falls-amid-prospects-for-reopening-of-strait-of-hormuz-79d7e674)

The strait carries on the order of a fifth of global seaborne oil. Iran has tightened practical control over transit since the wider war opened around late February 2026; Hormuz has been a central fault line in failed pauses, including an April ceasefire and a June memorandum that collapsed amid renewed fighting and competing claims over the waterway.[[3]](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/iran-oman-draft-waterway-deal-025457395.html)

### What officials say is on the table

Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Tuesday that progress had been made in talks involving Iran and Oman on increasing safe short-term traffic through Hormuz, "but not finality yet," and that Washington was "hoping that will happen very shortly." He described the United States as "involved in" the Iran–Oman conversation while casting full Iranian denuclearization as the "ultimate deal."[[2]](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/iran-war-donald-trump-us-denies-negotiations-begin-strikes-called-off/)

Axios, cited by multiple outlets and attributing two regional sources plus a U.S. official, reported the United States, Iran, and Oman were nearing a 60-day interim arrangement Washington hoped to announce as early as Wednesday. Under that reported draft: inbound traffic would use a northern lane in Iranian waters; outbound traffic a southern lane in Omani waters; no tolls or fees during the interim; and mine clearance from the median lane within about 30 days as a bridge toward a permanent two-way regime. Trump envoy Steve Witkoff was said to have contacted the Iranian and Omani foreign ministers. Sources told Axios Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi needed sign-off from Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei and the Supreme National Security Council; one U.S. official and a regional source said leadership approval had come through Tuesday.[[5]](https://www.jpost.com/international/article-904612)

A separate Iranian source familiar with the direct Iran–Oman track told CBS News the talks were focused on mechanics of reopening, with inbound coordination on the Iranian side, outbound on the Omani side, and a "service fee" with revenue split between Tehran and Muscat — a detail that sits in tension with fee-suspension language in the Axios account and is not independently confirmed as agreed text.[[2]](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/iran-war-donald-trump-us-denies-negotiations-begin-strikes-called-off/)

U.S. Central Command said the southern route "remains free and open for all commercial vessels" and that U.S. forces had assisted more than 1,000 vessels through the strait over three months. CENTCOM also said 45 commercial vessels heading into or out of Iranian ports had been redirected since a U.S. blockade was reinstated last month.[[2]](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/iran-war-donald-trump-us-denies-negotiations-begin-strikes-called-off/)

### Gaps and friction

None of this is a signed public agreement. Iran’s foreign ministry has denied direct negotiations with Washington even as Trump insists talks are real; Qatar, a mediator, has described work to create conditions for dialogue and said no direct Iran–U.S. talks were planned in the near term, while Emir Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al-Thani spoke with Trump Tuesday about de-escalation. Iranian state media and officials have cast doubt on any automatic link between an Iran–Oman shipping understanding and full reopening; a senior Gulf official told CNN the odds of an Iran–Oman deal by Friday were only "50-50," noting IRGC buy-in remains a binding constraint.[[3]](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/iran-oman-draft-waterway-deal-025457395.html)

Violence on the water did not pause for the rhetoric. Britain’s UKMTO reported a cargo vessel struck by an "unknown projectile" in the Strait of Hormuz off Oman; an Indian-linked vessel sank after an attack in the Red Sea near Yemen, with crew rescued. Yemen’s Houthis, aligned with Iran, have claimed repeated strikes on Saudi-linked tankers under a declared maritime blockade that pushes more Saudi crude toward Red Sea routes when Hormuz is constrained.[[3]](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/iran-oman-draft-waterway-deal-025457395.html)

### Bottom line

**Reported:** Trump’s Fox threat-and-timetable; Rubio’s "progress, not finality"; a sharp oil selloff to multi-week lows on reopening hopes; CENTCOM’s southern-lane and vessel-assist figures; continuing attacks on commercial shipping.

**Claimed, not locked:** a finished 60-day U.S.–Iran–Oman package ready for Wednesday announcement; fee structure; Iranian supreme-level final approval as operational fact rather than sourced assertion.

**Still open:** whether traffic volumes, insurance, and naval practice actually normalize — and whether another U.S. strike threat is deferred again or executed if the draft collapses. Markets moved on the former possibility. The strait’s status is still decided by instruments on the water, not by interview cadence alone.
